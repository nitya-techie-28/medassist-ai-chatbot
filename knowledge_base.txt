[SECTION: ONCOLOGY AND CANCER RECOGNITION]
Cancer is characterized by uncontrolled cellular proliferation and tissue invasion.
Warning Signs (CAUTION Criteria):
- C: Change in bowel or bladder patterns.
- A: A lesion or sore that fails to heal after 2-3 weeks.
- U: Unusual bleeding or abnormal discharge (e.g., hemoptysis, hematuria, postmenopausal bleeding).
- T: Thickening or palpable mass/lump in breast tissue, testicles, lymph nodes, or soft tissue.
- I: Indigestion, persistent dysphagia (swallowing difficulty), or early satiety.
- O: Obvious modification in preexisting mole/nevus (ABCDE: Asymmetry, Border irregularity, Color variation, Diameter > 6mm, Evolution over time).
- N: Nagging cough or persistent hoarseness.
Systemic B-Symptoms: Unintentional weight loss (>10% within 6 months), persistent night sweats, and recurrent unexplained fevers.
Clinical Protocol: Urgent referral for histological biopsy, tumor markers, and contrast cross-sectional imaging (CT, MRI, PET).

[SECTION: CARDIOVASCULAR EMERGENCIES AND HYPERTENSION]
Resting Blood Pressure Classifications:
- Normal: <120/<80 mmHg.
- Elevated/Stage 1: 130-139 / 80-89 mmHg.
- Stage 2: ≥140 or ≥90 mmHg.
- Hypertensive Crisis: Systolic >180 mmHg and/or Diastolic >120 mmHg.
Acute Coronary Syndrome (Myocardial Infarction): Retrosternal pressure, crushing chest pain radiating to left arm, neck, or jaw, diaphoresis, dyspnea, and nausea.
Emergency Action: Call emergency services immediately. Keep patient seated and calm. If non-allergic and advised by emergency dispatch, administer chewed Aspirin 300mg. Do not give sublingual nitrates if systolic BP < 90 mmHg.

[SECTION: NEUROLOGY AND ACUTE STROKE]
Acute Stroke Triage Protocol (BE-FAST):
- B (Balance): Sudden ataxia, loss of coordination, or severe vertigo.
- E (Eyes): Sudden visual field defect, diplopia, or monocular blindness.
- F (Face): Facial asymmetry or unilateral drooping.
- A (Arms): Unilateral motor weakness, pronator drift, or paralysis.
- S (Speech): Dysarthria, expressive or receptive aphasia.
- T (Time): Crucial therapeutic window. Intravenous thrombolysis (IV-rtPA) must be administered within 4.5 hours of last known normal. Mechanical thrombectomy indicated up to 24 hours in large vessel occlusions.
Immediate Action: Immediate CT/MRI neuroimaging to rule out intracranial hemorrhage prior to antiplatelet therapy.

[SECTION: RESPIRATORY DISTRESS AND ASTHMA]
Acute Bronchospasm & Asthma Exacerbation:
Symptoms: Expiratory wheezing, tachypnea (>25 breaths/min), intercostal retractions, inability to complete full sentences in one breath, pulsus paradoxus.
Emergency Management:
1. Inhaled Short-Acting Beta-2 Agonist (Salbutamol/Albuterol): 4-10 puffs via spacer every 20 minutes for the first hour.
2. Inhaled Ipratropium Bromide for severe exacerbations.
3. High-flow oxygen to maintain SpO2 between 93-95% (or 88-92% in chronic hypercapnic COPD patients).
4. Systemic oral corticosteroids (Prednisolone 40-50mg).
Call emergency services immediately if the chest becomes 'silent' (absence of wheezing due to severe airflow limitation) or if central cyanosis develops.

[SECTION: ENDOCRINOLOGY AND DIABETIC EMERGENCIES]
Hypoglycemia Management (<70 mg/dL / 3.9 mmol/L):
Symptoms: Tremors, diaphoresis, palpitations, neuroglycopenic confusion, and irritability.
"Rule of 15":
1. Consume 15g of fast-acting simple carbohydrates (150ml fruit juice, 3-4 glucose tablets).
2. Wait 15 minutes and repeat fingerprick glucose testing.
3. If still <70 mg/dL, repeat 15g intake. Once normalized, consume complex carbohydrates (bread, meal) to prevent rebound drops.
Unconscious Patient: Administer intramuscular Glucagon 1mg or IV Dextrose 10-25%; never place liquids in the mouth of an unresponsive patient.

[SECTION: GASTROENTEROLOGY AND ACUTE ABDOMEN]
Red-Flag Abdominal Presentations:
- Appendicitis: Periumbilical discomfort migrating to the right lower quadrant (McBurney's point tenderness, Rovsing sign positive, rebound tenderness, low-grade fever).
- Acute Pancreatitis: Severe epigastric pain radiating directly to the mid-back, exacerbated supine, accompanied by elevated serum lipase/amylase (>3x upper normal limit).
- Gastrointestinal Bleeding: Upper GI source presents as hematemesis (coffee-ground emesis) or melena (black, tarry stools). Lower GI source manifests as hematochezia (fresh red blood).
Protocol: Strict nil by mouth (NPO), fluid resuscitation with balanced crystalloids, and urgent surgical/gastroenterology consult.

[SECTION: DERMATOLOGY AND ACUTE BURNS]
Thermal Burn Triage:
- Superficial / Partial Thickness (1st & 2nd Degree): Erythema, blistering, exquisite tenderness.
Action: Irrigate under clean running lukewarm/cool water for at least 20 minutes immediately. Do not apply ice, butter, or oils. Cover loosely with sterile non-adherent dressing or clean plastic wrap. Do not debride intact blisters.
- Full Thickness (3rd Degree): Waxy white, leathery, painless (nerve endings destroyed).
Action: Urgent trauma/burn center admission. Calculate fluid resuscitation via Parkland formula (4mL x kg x %TBSA burn) for burns exceeding 15-20% total body surface area.

[SECTION: IMMUNOLOGY AND ANAPHYLAXIS]
Acute Anaphylactic Shock:
Clinical Signs: Rapid onset hives (urticaria), angioedema (lips, tongue, uvula), stridor, bronchospasm, hypotension, gastrointestinal cramping.
First-Line Therapy: Intramuscular Epinephrine (Adrenaline) 1:1000 dilution (0.5mg adult dose, 0.3mg pediatric) injected into the anterolateral mid-thigh immediately.
Secondary Interventions: High-flow oxygen, aggressive IV fluid boluses (Normal Saline 1-2L), antihistamines (H1/H2 blockers), and IV Hydrocortisone. Monitor for at least 6-8 hours due to risk of biphasic reaction.

[SECTION: PHARMACOLOGY AND MEDICATION TOXICOLOGY]
Common Drug Safety & Overdose Protocols:
- Paracetamol (Acetaminophen): Maximum adult daily ceiling is 4000mg (reduced to 2000-3000mg in chronic liver disease or elderly). Toxicity causes severe hepatotoxicity. Antidote: Intravenous N-acetylcysteine (NAC) guided by the Rumack-Matthew nomogram.
- Opioid Overdose: Classic triad of pinpoint pupils (miosis), respiratory depression (<8 breaths/min), and altered mental status. Antidote: Naloxone 0.4mg-2mg IV/IM/Intranasal, repeated every 2-3 minutes as required.
- NSAID Precautions: Avoid long-term Ibuprofen/Diclofenac in patients with peptic ulcer disease, chronic kidney disease (eGFR <30), or uncontrolled heart failure.
